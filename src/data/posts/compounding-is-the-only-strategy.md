1.01 to the power of 365 is about **37.8**. A tiny improvement, repeated daily, turns into a thirty-seven-fold result in a year.

The arithmetic is trivial. The discipline isn't — because for most of the curve, nothing seems to be happening.

## In code

The codebases I've enjoyed most weren't built in heroic rewrites. They improved a little with every pull request: a clearer name, a missing test, one fewer manual step in the deploy. None of those changes would impress anyone on its own. Together, they're the difference between a system people are afraid to touch and one they're happy to ship on a Friday.

Automating a CI/CD pipeline felt like that. No single step was dramatic. The sum was deployments that took about half the time and stopped needing a human to babysit them.

## In capital

Investing is the purest version of the idea. Returns earn returns. The biggest lever isn't brilliance; it's time in the market and not interrupting the process. That's the thinking behind MyDhira, too — practice the habit before the stakes are real.

## In the gym

Progressive overload is compounding with a barbell: add a little weight or one more rep, every week, and write it down. Miss a week and nothing breaks. Miss a year of weeks and everything does.

## The catch

Compounding punishes inconsistency more than it rewards intensity. One huge effort followed by a month off loses to a modest effort done every day. So I've stopped looking for big leaps and started protecting small streaks — the green squares on my GitHub graph included.

The curve in the Vision section isn't decoration. It's the plan.
